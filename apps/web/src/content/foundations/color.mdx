## Overview

Color in Veli is defined in three tiers on top of a primitive base. Primitives hold the raw palette. The tiers above reference those primitives, so a single change flows through every service that uses them.

This page documents the primitive layer: the fourteen palettes, how their steps work, why the values are written in OKLCH, and how to read and copy them. Brand, semantic, and component tokens are documented in Storybook, where each token can be seen against a live component.

## How it works

### Contrast is built into the steps

A color's step predicts its contrast. Put text at step 800 on a surface at step 200 and the pair clears WCAG AA for body text. The [contrast guidance](#contrast-by-step) explains the pairing rule.

### Reference, don't repeat

Each tier points at the tier below instead of holding its own value. Fix a primitive value in one place and the change reaches every service that uses it.

### Light and dark are equals

Every step ships a light and a dark value. A service changes appearance at the theme root instead of redefining tokens, so designers can choose a token by its role rather than its current appearance.

### Values are perceptual

Steps are spaced by how the eye reads lightness, not by even numeric jumps. Colors at the same step look comparably light across different hues, keeping all fourteen palettes in the same visual rhythm.

## Token architecture

Tokens are organised in three tiers over a shared primitive base. Each tier references the one below it rather than storing another raw value.

The reference chain shows how one primitive reaches a rendered component. Change `teal-800` once and the button label follows because each tier only holds a reference.

| Layer              | Holds                           | Named by       | Example                |
| :----------------- | :------------------------------ | :------------- | :--------------------- |
| Primitives         | Raw palette values in OKLCH     | Palette + step | `teal-800`             |
| Brand · Tier 1     | References to primitives        | Brand role     | `brand-primary-800`    |
| Semantic · Tier 2  | References to brand tokens      | Interface use  | `text-primary`         |
| Component · Tier 3 | References to semantic or brand | Component part | `button-primary-label` |

Fourteen primitive palettes cover the system: four near-neutral greys—slate, ash, stone, and sage—and ten chromatic ramps—blue, emerald, lime, green, yellow, orange, red, teal, indigo, and magenta. Every palette also has an alpha counterpart.

## Why OKLCH

OKLCH describes color using perceptual lightness (`L`), chroma (`C`), and hue (`H`). A token value such as `oklch(0.525 0.019 255)` can be read and adjusted along those three independent dimensions.

Unlike HSL or hex, OKLCH lightness closely follows human perception. Two hues with the same `L` appear similarly light. That property gives the scale an even rhythm and makes contrast predictable by step. OKLCH can also describe wide-gamut colors beyond sRGB, keeping the system accurate on displays that support Display P3.

## The 12-step scale

Every palette runs from step 100, the lightest role, to step 1200, the darkest. The values invert in dark mode while each step's role remains stable.

<ColorFoundationVisuals kind="scale" data-docs-markdown="omit" />

| Steps     | Role                     | Typical use                                       |
| :-------- | :----------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ |
| 100–200   | Surfaces                 | Page backgrounds, cards, and raised panels        |
| 300–400   | Interactive surfaces     | Hover, active, and selected fills                 |
| 500–700   | Borders and muted detail | Dividers, input borders, and disabled text        |
| 700       | Large text and icons     | Content needing 3:1 contrast on a light surface   |
| 800–900   | Body text and icons      | Content needing 4.5:1 contrast on a light surface |
| 1000–1200 | Emphasis and solid fills | Headings, strong labels, and inverse surfaces     |

These roles are guidance rather than a lock. The contrast relationship is what keeps an individual pairing accessible.

## Contrast by step

Contrast follows the gap between steps. Keep a text step and its surface step at least six steps apart and the pair meets WCAG AA's 4.5:1 target for normal text. Step 800 on step 200 is the reference pairing. Step 700 reaches the 3:1 large-text threshold on light surfaces, but not the 4.5:1 body-text threshold.

<ColorFoundationVisuals kind="contrast" data-docs-markdown="omit" />

Contrast must still be checked when color sits over imagery, transparency, gradients, or another surface that changes beneath it.

## Alpha palettes

Each palette ships with a matching alpha palette. Over the default surface, an alpha token is designed to read like its solid counterpart: `teal-alpha-200` and `teal-200` appear equivalent. Over another surface, the alpha variant allows the content behind it to influence the result.

Use alpha tokens for overlays, selected treatments, tinted imagery, and blurred panels. Prefer a solid token when the surface should remain predictable regardless of what sits behind it.

## Dark mode

Every primitive step has both light and dark values. In dark mode, lightness inverts while hue and role stay stable: `teal-200` remains a surface and `teal-800` remains readable foreground content.

Switch appearance once at the theme root. Do not select tokens by how they look in a single mode; select by role and let the correct value resolve for the active appearance.

## Guidelines

### Do

- Reference semantic or component tokens by role.
- Keep at least six steps between normal text and its surface.
- Use alpha tokens when the surface underneath should remain visible.
- Review every color decision in both light and dark modes.

### Don't

- Paste a raw OKLCH value directly into a component.
- Use step 700 as normal-size body text on a light surface.
- Put important text over decoration without a controlled surface.
- Use color as the only way to communicate meaning or state.

## Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA is the minimum for government services. The step system makes that outcome the default: maintain the six-step gap and normal body text passes without measuring every primitive pair. Large text—at least 18px bold or 24px regular—may use a pairing that meets the 3:1 threshold.

Color must never be the only signal. Pair a status color with a label, icon, or structural cue so its meaning survives for color-blind users and in grayscale. Keep text on a controlled surface rather than directly on decorative media.
